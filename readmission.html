Situation

US hospitals lose $17B a year on preventable readmissions. Most systems still operate reactively.

The readmission rate is not a measurement problem; it is a workflow problem. Discharge happens, PPR reports arrive 30–60 days later, penalties land two quarters after that. By the time a hospital knows, the patient is already back.

Reactive posture
Retrospective PPR reporting; no predictive signal at the point of discharge.

Quality teams see readmissions after they happen. Care managers triage by intuition, not by risk score. Every high-risk discharge without an intervention is a missed save.

Point-solution stack
LACE, ED-triage, and billing tools in separate silos; none are predictive at scale.

Hospitals buy a risk score, a dashboard, and a dashboard vendor. No shared data platform, no shared explainability layer, no shared financial model linking clinical work to HRRP exposure.

HRRP penalty drag
3% max Medicare payment penalty; compounds across fiscal years.

CMS HRRP penalizes above-expected readmission ratios automatically. For a mid-size system, that is $2–5M in annual exposure before any intervention cost is counted.

Four-Level Readmission Maturity

Advance one block at a time. Never skip.

Each level is defined in terms that are auditable, not aspirational. Most US hospital systems today sit at Level 1 or L2. The building-block principle is the governing constraint of the whole plan.

L1
Reactive
Retrospective PPR reporting; discharge checklists; LACE-style heuristics. Care managers triage on intuition. No predictive signal at the point of discharge.
Most hospitals today
L2
Risk-Stratified
Validated risk scores at discharge; manual care transitions; targeted follow-up calls. Explainability still thin; interventions generic across conditions.
Month 4–6
L3
Predictive + Monitored
XGBoost scoring with SHAP per patient; RPM on top-decile discharges; condition-specific interventions (CHF, COPD, pneumonia). HRRP financial simulator live.
Month 9–12
L4
Preventive at Scale
Automated interventions across all payers and conditions; continuous model retraining; agentic care-manager workflows; real-time HRRP exposure dashboards.
Beyond 12 mo.
Governing principle:Finish Level 2 before Level 3 opens. Deploying RPM without a calibrated risk model wastes capacity on low-risk patients. Deploying a risk model without RPM leaves the save unclaimed. The sequence is the strategy.
